# Do authentic data revisions change the decision? A real-time vintage study, corrected

**Draft research paper, version 2.** Branch `research/real-world-revision-study`. Version 1 of this
study was invalid for the model the protocol defines: the audit-time objective was constructed with the
wrong coefficient mapping (`z_A - 2 z_D` instead of `-z_A`). This version repairs the design and reports
which conclusions survive. The correction log — with every defect, its detection, its repair, its
verification and its numerical impact — is [CORRECTIONS.md](CORRECTIONS.md); the superseded results are
preserved unchanged under `artifacts/research/rtds/superseded/`.

Every number below is generated by the scripts in `scripts/research/rtds_*.py` and rendered into
`artifacts/research/rtds/tables.md`. The protocol and its amendment record are
[PREREGISTRATION.md](PREREGISTRATION.md).

## Abstract

Published macroeconomic observations are revised, and much is known about how large those revisions
are. Much less is known about whether they change *decisions*. We assemble 2,347 real-time vintage
responses (675 distinct release dates) over seven US series from ALFRED, forming 408 decision/audit
pairs spanning 2000–2025 in two separate revision processes, and run one fixed convex program on them,
measuring decision regret by re-solving with two independent solvers. Every audit-time program is
additionally built directly from the audit-vintage observations and asserted equal to the one produced
by the revision compiler (maximum disagreement 8.9e-16 over 408 events). In the confirmatory test split,
**73.7%** (95% clustered interval 41.7–91.7%) of quarterly BEA expenditure revisions and **50.0%**
(30.6–63.9%) of monthly BLS payroll revisions change the optimal decision by more than 1e-3 objective
units. A certificate computed from the original decision — the recorded weights and the multipliers
recorded with them — proves immateriality for 21.1% and 44.7% of events, covering 80.0% and 89.5% of the
revisions that were in fact immaterial, and never once certifies a material revision as immaterial. A
feasible descent supplies the opposite direction: a lower bound on regret that certifies materiality for
73.7% and 50.0% of events, leaving 5.3% honestly unknown in both cohorts. Three negative results matter
more than the positive ones. First, the certificate's certified set is **exactly** the set on which the
classical active-set/KKT stability test already holds, so it adds no classification power over a
one-line post-optimality test. Second, ranking revisions by numerical size is uninformative about
decision materiality (AUC 0.425 and 0.540), and decision geometry *without* a stability-radius diagnostic
is only moderately informative (0.675 and 0.737); the headline AUC of v1 (0.88/0.94) was carried by a
directional radius that costs 46 ms and 28–37 KKT checks per event, i.e. twenty times the cost of the
certificate and forty times an OSQP re-solve. Third, the stored-state advantage claimed in v1 does not
exist once the record is made sufficient: 736/673 bytes against 89/64 bytes for the marginal
representation a re-solve needs.

## 1. Question, and what is not claimed

*Of the authentic revisions examined, how many materially change the decision, and what can be
established without re-solving?*

The economic observations are real vintage data. **The decisions are not historical.** They are produced
by a convex program this study defines, so this is a counterfactual decision-model study, not an audit of
decisions anyone took. The growth-to-cost mapping is declared, not estimated from outcomes. Nothing here
is a new theorem: the certificate is weak duality plus an active-set argument, applied to real revisions.

## 2. Data and audit horizon

Source: **ALFRED**, the archival companion of FRED, read through `alfredgraph.csv?id=…&vintage_date=…`,
which returns the vintage series rather than the current one (verified before the protocol was written:
GDPC1 for 2014-10-01 reads 16,311.6 in the 2015-01-30 vintage, 16,294.7 in 2015-03-27 and 16,186.7 in
2016-07-29).

| cohort | frequency | series | vintage responses | train | pilot | test | holdout |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | quarterly | `GDPC1`, `GPDIC1`, `GCEC1`, `EXPGSC1` | 1,338 | 52 | 8 | 38 | 4 |
| B | monthly | `PAYEMS`, `MANEMP`, `USCONS` | 1,009 | 156 | 24 | 114 | 12 |

**Horizon (repaired).** The protocol specifies four calendar quarters (A) and twelve calendar months
(B) — the same calendar interval. The audit vintage is the first release on or after
`add_months(decision, 12)`, with month-end clipping. The earlier implementation used 400 and 366 days;
400 days is about thirteen months, which moved almost every quarterly audit vintage one release later.
98 of 98 paired quarterly events changed audit vintage; because an adjacent release usually carries the
same value for an old period, only 3 of them changed the standardised growth vector.

**Time policy.** A vintage date is a release date. It is treated as available at 23:59:59 UTC on that
date, and no intraday timestamp is claimed anywhere.

**Revision categories are not pooled.** Advance/second/later estimates and the annual-benchmark window
are labelled from the release calendar, and the two cohorts are analysed separately: pooling a quarterly
expenditure revision with a monthly payroll revision would be a statistic about the average of two
unrelated things. No revision is described as an error; a revision is a new estimate, not a correction of
a mistake.

**Accessibility findings.** The Philadelphia Fed's Real-Time Data Set for Macroeconomists was assessed
first and not used: its documented data-file paths now return the site's 404 page. `PCEC1` has no ALFRED
vintage archive, which is why the quarterly cohort uses the four real expenditure aggregates that do.
ALFRED's edge stalls on any non-default `curl` User-Agent and intermittently drops HTTP/2 streams; the
fetch uses the default agent, HTTP/1.1 and bounded retries.

**Rights.** ALFRED/FRED data are redistributed by the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis under its terms
of use; the underlying series are US government statistics in the public domain. This repository stores
derived event records, URLs and SHA-256 digests, not the vendor's raw files.

## 3. Design

**The convention, stated once.** The program is `min 0.5 x'Hx + c'x` with `H = rho·corr(z)` fixed across
vintages and `c = -z`. A revision is a change in the **linear cost vector**, applied with a positive
identity sensitivity: `delta = c_A - c_D = z_D - z_A`, so the compiled audit program satisfies
`c_D + delta == -z_A`. The two possible conventions (change in expected returns with a negative
sensitivity, or change in costs with a positive one) are never mixed.

**Runtime equivalence.** For every event the audit program is also built directly from the audit-vintage
observations, with no revision machinery involved, and asserted equal in objective coefficients, quadratic
matrix, bounds, budget, inequality rows and right-hand sides, and objective and gradient at the recorded
decision and four probe points. This is the guard against the v1 defect and is a permanent test.

**Program** (identical structure in both cohorts): `Σx = 1`, `0 ≤ x ≤ 0.6`, plus `x_gov ≥ 0.15`,
`x_inv ≤ 0.45` (A) or `x_manuf ≥ 0.20` (B). `c` is train-split standardised growth; `ρ = 1.0` by a
prespecified train-only rule that proved non-discriminating (see the amendment record), defended instead
by the sensitivity grid in §4.8.

**Three-way classification.** An upper bound below the tolerance proves immateriality. An upper bound
above it proves nothing, so materiality needs a lower bound, which comes from a feasible descent: a
budget-preserving swap search (closed-form steps, no solver) whose point satisfies `f(x_repair) ≥ f*`,
hence `f(x_D) - f(x_repair) ≤ regret`. Events with neither claim are reported as unknown.

**Statistics.** Moving-block bootstrap over decision years (the clustering unit), 2,000 resamples;
cluster bootstrap for AUC; one primary test per cohort, everything else secondary.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Revisions do change decisions, and more often than v1 reported

| cohort | test events | material at τ=1e-3 | 95% cluster interval | v1 (invalid model) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 38 | 24 → **28 (73.7%)** | [41.7%, 91.7%] | 63.2% |
| B | 114 | 54 → **57 (50.0%)** | [30.6%, 63.9%] | 47.4% |

Attribution (same events): with the corrected convention but the *old* audit vintages, A reads 27/38 and
B 57/114. So the sign error, not the horizon repair, accounts for most of the difference — and in cohort
B for all of it. Both lower bounds exceed the protocol's 10% threshold, so H1 holds in both cohorts.

### 4.2 Certification: coverage, abstention, and no false certificates

| cohort | certified immaterial | material witness | unknown | decisions certified | false immaterial | false material | immaterial events covered |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 8 (21.1%) [8.3%, 50.0%] | 28 (73.7%) | 2 (5.3%) | 94.7% | 0 | 0 | 8/10 |
| B | 51 (44.7%) [36.1%, 63.9%] | 57 (50.0%) | 6 (5.3%) | 94.7% | 0 | 0 | 51/57 |

Every test event has a nonzero revision, so these are genuine nonzero numerical changes whose decision
impact is provably below (or provably above) the threshold. The certificate never certifies a material
revision as immaterial, and the witness never fires on an immaterial one — the latter is structural, since
a lower bound cannot exceed the true regret, and it is asserted per event.

Note what the certified share does *not* mean: it is not accuracy, and v1's 92.1%/89.5% "accuracy"
figures are withdrawn (§C3 of the correction log). An upper bound above τ is a failure to prove, not a
proof of the opposite.

### 4.3 The certified set is exactly the classical active-set set

| cohort | certified and KKT-stable | certified but not stable | stable but not certified |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 8 | 0 | 0 |
| B | 51 | 0 | 0 |

This survives the correction unchanged, in both cohorts and with different counts. On authentic
revisions, CRI's certificate certifies an event exactly when the recorded weights remain KKT-optimal for
the revised program — which a post-optimality active-set test already tells you, in one least-squares
solve and one feasibility check, at 1.3 ms against the certificate's 2.0 ms. CRI is not wrong; it is
**redundant as a classifier**. Its extra content is the quantitative bound, which is valid even when the
decision is unstable — but that bound is loose where it matters (median bound/regret 4.26 and 5.26 on
material events, median bound 0.135 and 0.108 against a tolerance of 1e-3).

### 4.4 What predicts materiality — and what the old AUC was made of

| cohort | feature group | AUC | 95% cluster CI | radius used | median cost | KKT checks/event |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | magnitude | 0.425 | [0.138, 0.701] | no | ~0 ms | 0 |
| A | metadata | 0.537 | [0.414, 0.644] | no | ~0 ms | 0 |
| A | geometry, no radius | 0.675 | [0.489, 0.848] | no | ~0 ms | 0 |
| A | geometry + projection | 0.686 | [0.540, 0.821] | no | ~0 ms | 0 |
| A | full geometry | 0.911 | [0.779, 1.000] | **yes** | 46 ms | 28 |
| B | magnitude | 0.540 | [0.414, 0.697] | no | ~0 ms | 0 |
| B | metadata | 0.462 | [0.389, 0.534] | no | ~0 ms | 0 |
| B | geometry, no radius | 0.737 | [0.662, 0.823] | no | ~0 ms | 0 |
| B | geometry + projection | 0.750 | [0.671, 0.834] | no | ~0 ms | 0 |
| B | full geometry | 0.962 | [0.948, 0.983] | **yes** | 46 ms | 37 |

The claim "magnitude is uninformative" survives, and is stronger than in v1 (0.425/0.540, i.e. at or
below chance; v1 said 0.49/0.61). The claim "geometry matters" survives only in a weaker form: without
the directional stability radius, the AUC is 0.675 [CI includes 0.5] in cohort A and 0.737 in cohort B.
The v1 headline of 0.88/0.94 came from a feature that repeatedly re-runs the KKT test along the observed
revision direction — a target-proximal diagnostic, not free metadata. Charging its cost makes the point
concrete: 46 ms and 28–37 KKT checks per event, against 2.0 ms to certify and 1.1 ms for an OSQP
re-solve. Using it as a triage rule would be strictly worse than re-solving, so it is reported as a
diagnostic and not as a discovery.

The metadata group is also structurally limited here: at a twelve-month horizon every input is revised in
every event, so "number of changing inputs" has no variance.

### 4.5 CRI-U: still zero, now for a stated reason

| cohort | audit-base certificates | decision-base certificates | declared-box width (cost units) | actual correction outside the box |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0/38 | 0/38 | 0.28–0.36 | 50.0% of events |
| B | 0/114 | 0/114 | 0.19–0.29 | 50.9% of events |

The v1 implementation treated absolute cost levels as perturbations on a program with the wrong sign, so
its zero said nothing. Corrected, the result is the same zero, and the reason is now measurable: with
typical revisions of 0.2–0.36 cost units, a worst-case bound over a box of that size is hundreds of times
`τ = 1e-3`, and half the time the actual correction is not even inside the declared box. A worst-case
certificate at this tolerance is not a useful object for revisions of this size. Verification of the
machinery itself (bound sound against exhaustive vertex enumeration, singleton-at-zero reproducing CRI,
level-as-centre inflating the bound) is in `tests/test_rtds_design.py`.

### 4.6 Cost and stored state

| cohort | certificate | active-set | compile | repair | internal re-solve | warm re-solve | OSQP re-solve | radius search |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 2.06 ms | 1.30 ms | 0.26 ms | 0.21 ms | 4.20 ms | 4.25 ms | **1.14 ms** | 43.21 ms |
| B | 2.00 ms | 1.30 ms | 0.25 ms | 0.13 ms | 4.55 ms | 4.54 ms | **1.08 ms** | 40.84 ms |

Certification beats the project's own SLSQP re-solve (2.0 vs 4.2–4.6 ms) and its warm start, and loses to
OSQP by about 2×. That ordering is unchanged from v1 and is the honest summary: if the model and a
re-solve are available, re-solving is cheaper.

**Stored state (bytes per decision).**

| cohort | sufficient audit record | shared block (once) | amortized shared | record + amortized | audit cost vector only | cost vector + amortized | full serialised problem |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 736 | 634 | 6.2 | 742.2 | 83 | 89.2 | 1,080 |
| B | 673 | 390 | 1.3 | 674.3 | 63 | 64.3 | 807 |

The v1 storage claim is withdrawn. A *sufficient* record must carry the weights, the multipliers and the
decision-time cost vector; once the shared structure is amortized, that is 742/674 bytes against 89/64
bytes for the marginal representation a re-solve needs. The record is bigger, not smaller. What it offers
is different and narrower: a fresh process reconstructs and verifies the certificate from the record, the
shared block and the newly available revision data alone — demonstrated for 376 records with a worst
bound gap of 3.6e-15 (`scripts/research/rtds_audit_record.py --verify`).

### 4.7 Untouched later period, 2024-07-01 .. 2025-06-30

| cohort | events | material | certified immaterial | material witness | unknown |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 4 | 25.0% | 75.0% | 25.0% | 0% |
| B | 12 | 58.3% | 33.3% | 58.3% | 8.3% |

These are the first computations on that period, with every parameter fixed on the train split and no
selection performed on it. They are nevertheless **descriptive, not confirmatory**: the sessions that
produced them came after the test sample had been inspected, and cohort A's four events cannot distinguish
25% from 75%. They are consistent with the test split and support nothing on their own.

| A | 0.02 | 26.3% | 34.2% | 34.2% | 65.8% |
| A | 0.2 | 31.6% | 44.7% | 50.0% | 50.0% |
| A | 1.0 | 47.4% | 73.7% | 73.7% | 21.1% |
| B | 0.02 | 25.4% | 28.1% | 31.6% | 68.4% |

Materiality and certifiability both depend on the risk weight: with a nearly linear objective the
decision sits on a corner, revisions rarely move it, and certification rises to 66–68%. The qualitative
conclusions hold across the grid; the percentages do not.

## 5. What survived, what did not

**Survived**

1. Revisions change decisions often: 50.0% and 73.7% of confirmatory events, with both cluster intervals
   excluding 10%.
2. The certificate is sound (zero false immateriality certificates, in both cohorts, under both versions)
   and reasonably complete (80% and 89.5% of immaterial events).
3. The certified set is exactly the classical active-set set — a redundancy result that survived the
   correction with different counts and different data.
4. Revision magnitude is uninformative about materiality, at or below chance.
5. CRI-U certifies nothing at this tolerance, now with a mechanism rather than an artefact.
6. Certification is slower than an OSQP re-solve and faster than the project's own SLSQP.

**Weakened or withdrawn**

7. "Geometry predicts materiality with AUC 0.88/0.94" — **weakened**. Without the directional radius it is
   0.675/0.737, and with it the method costs 46 ms per event.
8. "The audit record is 366–429 bytes, a large storage saving" — **withdrawn**. A sufficient record is
   736/673 bytes and larger than the marginal state a re-solve needs.
9. "CRI accuracy 92.1%/89.5%" — **withdrawn**. It counted uninformative upper bounds as materiality
   claims.
10. All v1 materiality, certification and AUC figures computed on `z_A - 2 z_D` — **invalid**, preserved
    as superseded.

## 6. Threats to validity

* **Decisions are counterfactual.** No historical operational decision is audited.
* **The mapping is declared, not identified.** `c = -z` with train-split standardisation; §4.8 varies the
  risk weight, not the functional form.
* **Sample size.** 38 test events and 10 clusters in cohort A; the interval says so. The holdout for A has
  four events.
* **Reanalysis, not confirmation.** The 2015–2024H1 sample had been inspected before these corrections, so
  its corrected results are a reanalysis. The 2024H2–2025H1 sample is untouched but tiny in A.
* **Dependence.** Events within a cohort share release calendars and macro shocks; the block bootstrap over
  years is a partial correction.
* **Fixed horizon.** Twelve calendar months is one choice; revisions continue afterwards, and at this
  horizon every input is revised in every event.
* **Vendor dependence.** ALFRED's archive is the measurement instrument, and a series' vintage history can
  itself be reconstructed by the vendor.
* **Category labels** are calendar heuristics, not agency-published classifications.
* **Small dimension.** Four and three assets; the KKT test's behaviour in higher dimensions is not measured
  here.
